We cannot understand Pentecost without the Old Testament. Thus we begin by looking at Pentecost in both the Old and New Testaments.

Part 1

Pentecost in the Old Testament

✓ Feast of Weeks — Chag Shavuot

It is called the Feast of Weeks because God commanded the people to count exactly seven weeks (49 days) from the harvest offering during the Feast of Unleavened Bread, and then celebrate on the 50th day.

Leviticus 23:15–16

Count seven full weeks — then celebrate on the 50th day.

How It Was Celebrated

According to Deuteronomy 16:9–12, the celebration had a few distinct characteristics:

🎉 A Joyful, Inclusive Gathering

It was a day of sacred assembly where no regular work was permitted. Deuteronomy explicitly emphasizes that this wasn't just for the religious elite; it was a massive, joyful community feast meant to include family, servants, foreigners, orphans, and widows.

⛓️ Remembering Slavery

God reminded the Israelites during this festival that they were once slaves in Egypt (Deuteronomy 16:12). Celebrating the harvest was a direct reminder that God had delivered them from bondage and given them a rich, fertile land of their own.

Deuteronomy 16:12

Remember that you were slaves in Egypt, and follow carefully these decrees.

Part 2

Pentecost in the New Testament

In the New Testament — 400 years later:

  • Greek became the common global language.
  • Greek-speaking people started calling the holiday Pentecost, which literally means "Fiftieth Day" in Greek.
  • So it was already an annual Jewish festival.
  • Jesus stayed with them for forty days after His resurrection (Acts 1:3). The command was very simple: do not leave Jerusalem until something happens. So they stayed for 10 days praying to God, waiting for the festival.

What Happened on This Day

God deliberately used this specific festival to do something completely new. They were all together in one place. Three extraordinary things happened:

🌬️

1. A Violent Wind

A sound like the blowing of a violent wind came from heaven and filled the house. The Greek word for wind is spirit. His presence as a Spirit filled the place.

Acts 2:2
🔥

2. Tongues of Fire

A fulfilment of Numbers 11:29. It never filled only some people. It never chose who to fill. It filled everybody — the young, the old, the educated, the uneducated.

Acts 2:3 · Numbers 11:29
🗣️

3. Different Languages

People spoke different languages but they understood each other. This was the direct reversal of the Tower of Babel (Genesis 11), where God confused human languages to scatter people. At Pentecost, God used languages to bring people together.

Acts 2:4 · Genesis 11

They could not understand how. Some started saying that they were drunk. That is when Peter spoke — already filled with the Holy Spirit — and boldly began to preach.

Acts 2:14–41

  • Peter declares that what the crowd sees — the disciples speaking in many languages — is not drunkenness, but the fulfilment of Joel's prophecy (Joel 2:28–32).
  • He then shifts the message and preaches about Jesus of Nazareth — a man accredited by God. He performed miracles, but when He was handed over to them, they nailed Him on the cross.
  • And let it be known that God raised Him from the dead and He is now seated at His right hand. Peter quoted the words of David in Psalms 16 and 110.
  • He made it known that God has made this Jesus, Lord and Messiah.

It was a bold sermon, grounded in the Scriptures and empowered by the Spirit of God. They asked Peter what they could do and the message was simple:

"Repent and be baptized."

Around 3,000 were baptized that day

This marked the birth of the church. Thus it is correct to say that Pentecost is the birthday of the church.

Application

3 Lessons About This Experience

1

Tarry in Jerusalem Until He Comes

Acts 1:4

Waiting is not passive. It is a time of active preparation, prayer, and expectation. Pentecost is also calling us believers to be patient and wait for His timing.

The disciples had no idea when the Holy Spirit would come. But the wait was worth it.

"At His time, He will give you a new song."
2

We Really Need the Holy Spirit

Acts 2:2–4

We cannot keep operating on our weaknesses. The disciples and the apostles were hiding in those corners until the Holy Spirit came down upon them. And remember — this Peter is the one who denied Jesus three times. But after receiving the Holy Spirit, he speaks boldly of this Gospel.

In a changing world, we need men and women who are Spirit-filled. Who shall speak with boldness of what the Spirit wants people to hear — and not what they want to hear.

He is the source of empowerment. He fills people, not the building.

"He fills people, not the building."
🔥 Are you Spirit-filled?
3

We Need Each Other

Acts 2:42 ff

Spiritual breakthroughs thrive in an atmosphere of unity. There is something about joining forces that moves spiritual mountains.

The language miracle symbolizes the connectedness God desires for His people; language is meant to bind people together for a common purpose.

What is striking about Acts 2 is that it begins with togetherness and it ends with togetherness. Acts 2:42 ff speaks of shared teaching, shared meals, shared prayers, even shared possessions.

The Holy Spirit united their hearts in singleness of purpose and mission. Don't you long for that kind of unity in the Church today? Simply, we cannot accomplish individually the mission that God has given us.

Acts 2:42

They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer.

"You can't pick up a grain of rice with just one finger."
